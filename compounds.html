Ions, Molecules and Salts

The chemical species everyone meets, kind by kind
Abstract The commonly known species of chemistry, from the proton to chlorophyll: cations and anions, inorganic and organic molecules, acids and bases with their pKa or pKb, oxides with their acid or basic character, and salts with their solubility in water. Each is shown as in a reference table: its kind, its formula, its name and its molar mass, computed from the standard atomic weights, with its composition by mass and what it is known for. Every element in a composition opens the element’s page.

    Notes

    Molar masses are computed from the IUPAC standard atomic weights, abridged, and are given in g·mol⁻¹; for an ion the mass of the electrons gained or lost is neglected. pKa and pKb are for water at 25 °C, one value per proton for polyprotic acids; the pKa of strong acids are estimates, since they dissociate completely in water. Solubilities are for water at room temperature: insoluble means less than about 0.1 g per 100 mL. Minerals, the crystalline compounds of nature, have their own page in Geology: Minerals and their crystals.
