Minerals and their Crystals

What rocks are made of, and how their atoms are stacked
Abstract A mineral is a naturally occurring solid with a definite chemical composition and an ordered arrangement of atoms: a crystal. Its atoms repeat a small motif, the unit cell, along three directions, and the shape of that cell sorts every crystal into one of seven crystal systems and fourteen Bravais lattices, shown first in 3D. Then the common minerals, by chemical class, each with its formula, molar mass and composition, its crystal system, lattice and space group, and the properties used to tell it apart in the field: hardness on Mohs’ scale, density, lustre, colour, streak, cleavage and habit.

    6.1.1Crystal Systems and Bravais Lattices

    A crystal is a lattice of points, all with identical surroundings, with the same group of atoms (the motif) placed on every point. The lattice is built by repeating a parallelepiped, the unit cell, of edges a, b, c and angles α (between b and c), β (between a and c) and γ (between a and b). The symmetry of the cell sorts crystals into seven systems. Besides the primitive cell, with points only at its corners, some systems admit centred cells, with an extra point at the centre of the body (I), of every face (F) or of one pair of faces (C): Bravais showed in 1848 that there are exactly fourteen distinct lattices in three dimensions.

    Notes

    Hardness is on Mohs’ scale, a ranking by which mineral scratches which, from talc (1) to diamond (10): gypsum 2, calcite 3, fluorite 4, apatite 5, orthoclase 6, quartz 7, topaz 8, corundum 9. The streak is the colour of the powder, left by rubbing on unglazed porcelain, and is more reliable than the colour of the crystal. Cleavage is the tendency to split along planes of weak bonding, given by their Miller indices {hkl}. Space groups are in Hermann–Mauguin notation; the first letter gives the centring of the lattice. Formulas are idealised end members: natural crystals take in other elements. Densities are in g/cm³. The compounds of chemistry are in Ions, Molecules and Salts.
